# The interface and TRACT reviewed, 2026-11-11

On 2026-11-11 the maintainer asked (through the o3-303 session) for a Fable review of the vocoder and its interface, for GitHub issues #2, #3
and #4 to be fixed along with whatever Fable found, and later for Fable's view of what the TRACT mode could do. This file records the
two reviews, the maintainer's hands-on notes from v0.2.0, what has been built since, and what is still open, in the order it will be built.
Where this file and the code disagree, the code and the commit messages win. Every cost marked [est] is an estimate until the unit reads it.

## 1. The maintainer's hands-on notes (v0.2.0 on the unit)

- **PATTERN: a single note was very hard to enter.** Only F5 VALUE and the encoder gave one; the pads did not make much sense on PATTERN.
  Issue #2's fix (1ff2d3e, SHIFT + a chord pad writes the root alone) helps, but it is a modifier on a grid that still writes blind.
  Section 3 is the redesign.
- **The slots' loops did not play well.** Two causes are fixed:
  - Issue #3 (14e9ecf): a slice paused the slot's whole loop, and the loop resumed one slice behind when the slice ended. On the host,
    the loop came back at about 14.68 s on 250 Hz material where an unbroken loop would have been at 300 Hz. TRIG also defaulted to GATE,
    so a short tap cut the slice short.
  - Fable's finding 2 (1f7bd9c): a take recorded from PERFORM only moved forward on PERFORM, PARAMS and the SAMPLE screens. On
    PATTERN, SONG, MASTER or FILE it recorded until you came back (up to 16 s), and the slot did not take its side.
  - A different fault was not covered by these: a loop drifting against the tempo, a click at the seam, a late start at the bar. Slice LS1
    (unreleased) re-syncs whole-bar loops at each bar line, refuses a take that cannot fit and ramps a stop over 64 samples; a late start at
    the bar (a press just after the line) is still open, and all of it needs more testing on a unit.

## 3. The pads, redesigned (P1, Q1 to Q9)

The problem, read against the code:

- A palette pad writes into the cursor's step and moves on, and **nothing sounds**: you write what you cannot hear.
- On the NOTE lane both palette rows are chords. A single note takes SHIFT (1ff2d3e) or PADS on KEYS on another screen (PERFORM's F7).

The maintainer's idea on 2026-11-11: fewer playing rows, and an F-key that chooses how the pads play (a note, a power chord, a 7th...).
Fable 5.2 was asked, free to rethink it, and this is its design. Two of its claims were checked against the code: SMOOTH, the default
voicing, picks the octave nearest the last chord and ignores the pad's (`chords.h:186-204`); PATTERN's F row is full
(`pattern_screen.cpp:29`). The maintainer can overrule it before or after it is built.

**The layout: two F-keys, ROW and PLAYS** (the maintainer's refinement, 2026-11-11: every permutation of the pads with two F-keys, a
different chord kind on each row, KEYS' controls always there). Fable's "notes above, F6's mode below" becomes the default of a layout
the hands set:

- Three rows are assignable: A, B and C (PERFORM's rows 1, 2 and 3). **ROW** chooses one; its LEDs flash once when ROW changes.
  **PLAYS** sets what the chosen row plays: NOTE, TRIAD, 7TH, OPEN, POWER or OFF. Row C also offers FORMANT, BEND and, in TRACT, MOUTH
  (SHIFT + F7 BEND ROW folds into it). A tap focuses, a second tap steps and wraps, the encoder goes both ways and stops at the ends.
- The rows are independent, so every permutation is there: a different chord on each row, two rows of notes, three rows of chords.
- A column is a root, C on the left. A row above another plays one octave higher, so two NOTE rows are two chromatic octaves and two
  rows of one chord kind are the same chords an octave apart.
- **SMOOTH stays and keeps to the pad's octave** (the maintainer asked whether it is worth keeping, 2026-11-11). SMOOTH, the default
  voicing, picks the arrangement nearest the chord before, so a progression moves without jumps. Today it searches every octave and
  ignores the pad's (`chords.h:186-204`), so two rows of one kind would sound the same. From Q1 it searches only the inversions in
  the pad's own octave: a progression along a row still moves smoothly, and a row higher sounds higher. A SMOOTH step already in a
  pattern keeps its root's octave, so it may land an octave from where it sounds today; the ear decides.
- The default is A NOTE, B TRIAD, C FORMANT: Fable's design, a single note one press away.
- What a row plays is the step's stored kind (0 a note, 1 to 4 `ChordKind` + 1), so on PATTERN a row is a brush and a pattern mixes
  kinds.
- **One layout for the project:** set on any screen that has ROW and PLAYS, shown by every screen, saved with the project.

**Where the rows sit:**

| Screen | Rows that play | The other rows | ROW and PLAYS |
|---|---|---|---|
| PERFORM | 1, 2, 3 (A, B, C) | Row 0: KEYS' controls (OCT, VOL, ROOT, MOD, BND), always, in every layout | F6 ROW, F7 PLAYS, in place of CHORD and PADS |
| PATTERN | 0 and 1 (A and B, the palette) | Rows 2 and 3: steps and patterns, as today | F6 ROW (A or B), F7 PLAYS. CLR LANE moves to SHIFT + F6 with its F1 confirm, FOLLOW to SHIFT + F7 (PATTERN's SHIFT row is empty today) |
| SAMPLE | 2 and 3 (A and B) | Rows 0 and 1: slices | F1 PLAYS, SHIFT + F1 ROW (its F row is full, its SHIFT row empty). SAMPLE stops following PERFORM's page: finding 15 (P4) closes here |

- The controls row is on PERFORM only: PATTERN and SAMPLE need their other two rows for steps and slices. PATTERN's octave is UP and
  DOWN; SAMPLE plays at PERFORM's.
- ROW and PLAYS are dim on PATTERN's CARRIER, LEVEL, SLICE and MOUTH lanes, as QUALITY is outside TALK.
- **A shortcut for live play:** a pad of a row held + PLAYS (a tap or the encoder) changes that row at once, without ROW, and ROW
  follows it. ROW stays the way the screen shows and HELP lists.
- **What the layout costs, accepted:** PERFORM has three chord rows at most where CHORDS has four today (the controls row is always
  there); PATTERN and SAMPLE show A and B only, so a kind kept on C is written from PATTERN by putting it on A or B; SAMPLE's ROW and
  PLAYS sit on F1, not F6 and F7, because TRIG and CHOKE keep their keys there; CLR LANE and FOLLOW move to PATTERN's SHIFT row.
- **PIANO stays, for chords too** (the maintainer, 2026-11-11). A and B can join into one piano, black keys on A and white keys on B,
  as PADS on KEYS today. PLAYS on A or B offers PIANO NOTE, PIANO TRIAD, PIANO 7TH, PIANO OPEN and PIANO POWER: every key plays that
  kind on its own root. A grid value on A or B splits them again; the other row gets back its last grid value. Two F-keys still reach
  everything, on every screen, PATTERN's palette included.
- **SHIFT, one rule: the pad without its consequence.** SHIFT + a step pad writes a rest (as today); SHIFT + a palette pad sounds and
  writes nothing. PERFORM's latch (SHIFT tapped while a pad is held) stays.
- **LEDs:** the playing rows light as PERFORM's do today (the key's root low, in the scale dim, held on), PATTERN's palette included
  (it is all dim today). Mid marks the pad that would write the cursor's step as it already is.
- **Step entry on PATTERN:** a palette press sounds while held, writes the cursor's step on the press and moves the cursor on. A step pad
  held + a palette pad writes that step and the cursor stays. The audition goes through PERFORM's local-note path, so the sequencer's
  note-offs do not cut it. PATTERN gains a `leave()` that lets held notes go (a screen button leaves at any moment).
- **UP and DOWN on PATTERN become the octave** (today they repeat the lane buttons and F2).
- **The other lanes:** LEVEL sounds the step's note at that level; SLICE fires the slice; CARRIER stays silent.
- **What goes:** `kind_for_row`'s four-kind grid (1ff2d3e), SHIFT + a chord pad writing one note, PERFORM's F6 CHORD and F7 PADS as
  they are, SHIFT + F7 BEND ROW (into PLAYS), and the piano page as a rule of its own (PIANO is now a PLAYS value).
- **Not taken:** Fable's fixed rows (now the layout's default) and its F6 PADS brush (one F-key cannot give a kind per row); the idea
  as first said, one mode on every row (rows that repeat under SMOOTH, and a single note costs a mode change in a chord line); today's
  grid of a kind per row (no notes, a different row count per screen, no room for controls or the strip); mode pads on the grid (the
  LEDs cannot label them, and they take a row from PATTERN's steps); a sixth MIX value (only if the unit shows the four-kind grid is
  missed); a vowel pad mode on F6 (the strip and the MOUTH lane cover it).
- **The project:** the layout is three bytes in P3's appended chunk. An older file maps KEYS to PIANO NOTE, C FORMANT (BEND with BEND
  ROW), and CHORDS to the kinds `kind_for_row` gave rows 1 to 3 (C BEND with BEND ROW).

## 4. TRACT: fit and ideas (Fable, read-only; built from 2026-11-11 as V1 to V4)

The plan is the o3-303 session's (`../o3-303/docs/TRACT-KNOBS-PLAN.md`) and so is the DSP (`../o3-sdk/extras/tract`, committed with X3 in
bb2bd18: 256 cycles a frame at Full, below the 390 counted here). This app builds the mode on top of it as V1 to V4 (section 5); a change
the tube needs goes through that session. The notes below were written while TRACT was paused.

**Fit.** It fits the 60 % budget with about a third of the room the plan's section 4.6 claims. The tube counted from the MK66
disassembly is 390 cycles a frame (25.0k a block, 31.2k with the plan's 1.25), not 175. Fable's base is the unit's TALK reads
(`ENGINE-REALISM.md`), with TALK's own analysis (about 15k ± 5k) taken out and the tube added:

| Load | Cycles [est] | % of a block | Plan 4.6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idle | 92k to 98k | 35 to 38 | 21 to 25 |
| 4 notes | 109k to 115k | 42 to 44 | not given |
| 8 notes | 126k to 132k | 48 to 51 | about 40 |
| 8 notes, full carrier plan | 132k to 138k | 51 to 53 | not given |
| 8 notes and 4 loops | 153k to 159k | 59 to 61 | not given |

- A mode change with 8 notes already costs 76 to 88 % of its block. With the tube it would be 86 to 98 % [est]. The tract's MIX should
  start after the old engine's ramp ends, and "a mode change with 8 notes and 4 loops" belongs on the unit list.
- The tube runs with no note held (the processor sleeps only at MIX 0). A sleep on silent input with a decayed tube would bring idle back to
  about 67k [est].
- Lite saves only about 5k. It is not worth its formants.
- The second unit with ten ids is right; the Processor is at most 1,536 B (about 2 KB with its unit) of the 9,784 B SRAM margin. The
  MOUTH lane as a parallel byte array is sound, and the pattern reader skips unknown chunks, so `MOUT` can be added.

**Corrections to the plan:**
- A closure is wet silence and a pop. The closure pads are a mute with a plosive, never m or n (there is no nose branch).
- `set_closure` takes a bool, so HISS sounds only in the 12 ms of travel or on very narrow vowels. "p, t, k with NOISE" rests on the
  carrier's NOISE, not on HISS.
- A sequenced slide that rewrites GLIDE fights the knob and the project's save. An appended SLIDE id is better.
- The plan should say that the tube takes the carrier bus after the CarSrc switch (NOISE, LOOP 1..4, LIVE, VOICE), and test it.
- "CHORDS row 0 = the power chords" is only true for F6 TRIAD since 1ff2d3e. Rows follow `kind_for_row` now.
- The mode-count list is longer than the plan's:
  - `modes.h`
  - `vocoder.cpp` (names and `ramps[4]`)
  - `perform_logic.h` (`mode_page`, `kModePages`, `kF8`)
  - the `bars` tests in `perform_screen.cpp` and `params_screen.cpp`
  - the console's usage text
  - `presets.h`
  - `modes_test.cpp`

**Ideas, best value for the cost first:**

1. **The tube on any SOUNDS source**: a slot, the live input, noise. A drum loop or a guitar speaks. No new DSP and no extra CPU beyond the
   loops' own; one sentence and a test in V1.
2. **Words in the MOUTH lane**: short gesture tables the hook plays inside a step: WOW (U>A>U), YEAH (I>E>A), YOI, WAH, OW, BA, DA, GA.
   - It needs no DSP, and costs about 1.5 % while the shape moves [est]. It fits V3.
   - Within reach: vowels, diphthongs, w and y, and b d g p t k.
   - Out of reach: nasals, l and r (three numbers do not steer F3), and a held s.
   - The result is sung words a listener recognises when told what they are, not speech.
3. **A tempo-synced mouth LFO and random babble**: VOWEL ramped between two places at 1/4 to 1/32 (the talking-bass "yoi"), or a seeded
   random walk that fills the lane. App only, low risk.
4. **Accent opens the jaw**: the step's LEVEL or the note's pitch moves JAW. A few lines in the hook.
5. **FORMANT in TALK by resampling the tube** (BUILT as TF1, 2026-11-12: `docs/ENGINE-REALISM.md`, "TALK's FORMANT, TF1"; best within about +-6, down weaker, QUALITY 8 only going up), the cheap half of "the singer's mouth plays the tube":
   - TALK ignores FORMANT today.
   - Its Levinson recursion already gives reflection coefficients (order 12 at Quality 16, fs/4, a 128-frame hop), and its lattice is a
     Kelly-Lochbaum tube.
   - Once a hop: coefficients to areas, the area function resampled (a longer or shorter mouth), back to coefficients. That is gender
     and size on a talkbox.
   - It costs about 0.3k a block [est] and reuses parameter id 40.
   - Risk: with the pre-emphasis the areas are not physical. A host test on formant positions decides. Its own slice, after TRACT.
6. **The live LPC plays the 22-section tube.** A tube at fs/2 with twice the sections, the coefficients on every second junction,
   matches up to 5.5 kHz.
   - It needs an area or reflection API on the Processor, a test of the end conditions and the sign, the analysis and the tube in one
     unit (the View's 18 coefficients only move at the main loop's rate), and about 10k more (8 notes at 52 to 55 % [est]).
   - The most original instrument here (a talkbox you can resize, freeze and pull to vowels), with the highest risk. After idea 5.
7. **Capture the mouth** (BUILT as MC1, 2026-11-12: PATTERN, MOUTH lane, SHIFT + F8, and `mcap` in the console; needs the unit and real voices): a slot's voice analysed offline into the MOUTH lane (the nearest of the nine vowel places by log-area
   distance, a closure where the level dips), replayed on any carrier at any tempo. No real-time cost; O/U and E/I will be confused on
   real voices. After idea 5.
8. **A choir** (a second tube at another SIZE): 20k to 25k more, 8 notes at 56 to 60 % [est]. Dropped for now.
9. **TRACT as BANDS's modulator**: dropped. A vocoder copies the modulator's envelope onto the carrier, which is what TRACT already does
   to the same carrier, sharper. Recording TRACT into a slot covers the curious case.

Fable's recommendation:
- Add to V1 to V4: the CPU table above, the sequenced mode change on the unit list, a sleep on silence, the full mode-count list and the
  tab width, "any SOUNDS source" stated and tested, a SLIDE id, and in V3 the words, the synced LFO, the random fill and accent-to-jaw.
- Later: TALK's FORMANT by area resampling, then the LPC-driven tube, then capturing the mouth.
- Drop: the BANDS route and the choir.

### TRACT in PATTERN (Fable, 2026-11-11)

Yes, as plan 4.4's MOUTH lane: a line of its own per pattern, the note step word untouched (o3-303's decision 3, the vocoder's way).

- **MOUTH is a fifth lane beside SLICE, not in its place** (the maintainer, 2026-11-11: "I want SLICE too; in PATTERN I'd like to mix
  many things"). This overrules plan 4.4 and Fable, which put MOUTH in SLICE's place. The lanes are NOTE, CARRIER, LEVEL, SLICE, MOUTH:
  F2 LANE taps through all five, Fx goes to SLICE and a second Fx goes to MOUTH (and back). Outside TRACT the MOUTH lane is shown dim
  and kept, "MOUTH SPEAKS IN TRACT", as QUALITY is outside TALK. In TRACT the slices play on; whether a slice goes through the mouth
  follows SOUNDS (a slot as the carrier), settled when V1 is designed. A fifth lane row costs display-list words: measure them in the
  slice that adds it.
- The palette: row 0 the mouth strip, the same columns as PERFORM's row 3 in TRACT (nine vowel places, then LIPS, TIP, BACK); row 1 the
  same with glide. One home for the strip ends plan 4.2's split between "KEYS row 3" and "CHORDS row 0".
- The gestures are the notes': a pad moves the mouth while held, writes and moves on; a step held + a pad writes that step; SHIFT + a pad
  is live only; SHIFT + a step is FOLLOW. On PERFORM with the line playing, a held strip pad overrides it and the release gives it back
  (o3-303's decision 6). F6 is dim on MOUTH: the notes and chords are the glottis.
- One byte a step: bits 0 to 3 are 0 FOLLOW, 1 to 9 the vowels, 10 to 12 the closures, 13 to 15 kept for the words; bit 7 is GLD
  (replacing the plan's 13 to 21). The hook writes an appended SLIDE id, not GLIDE.
- The cost: 16 B a pattern, 128 B a bank, in the SDRAM (9 B more for a length and LINE on/off per pattern, as on the 303); a `MOUT` chunk
  of about 131 B, an older file loads as FOLLOW; `Clip`, COPY, INSERT and DELETE carry the mouth bytes; at most three Param events a
  step. The unit decides the closures' and GLD's sound, and the CPU of a sequenced mode change with 8 notes and 4 loops.
