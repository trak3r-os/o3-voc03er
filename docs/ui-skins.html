VOC03ER Skins

Three ways to dress the accepted layout. SPEAKS, VOICEPRINT, SOUNDS, the pads drawing and the F row stay where they are; what changes is how they look.

Recommendation: OR NOIR as the default. It makes the voiceprint the centre of the instrument, it keeps the accepted structure untouched, and its frame costs 1,140 words, close to the design's estimate of about 1,100.

All three can ship as a selectable option. The limit is test and unit work, not memory: a skin is under 100 B of theme, a few KB of flash and one settings byte, and only the active skin's bitmaps need to sit in RAM_G. Each one adds its ten screens to the scenarios and to the unit check, so build the seam and OR NOIR first, then COLUMN 80, which is the cheapest, and HALFTONE last, which needs a new drawing path.

Gold speaks and chrome sounds, on a black wall of round LEDs, so the voiceprint stands in the middle of the screen like a stage display.

What makes it this skin

  • The pinhole mask. One 1-bit bitmap, tinted the colour of the wall, turns any flat bar into round 5 px LEDs: voiceprint, IN and OUT all pass under it.
  • Metal from flat bands. The wordmark is the same string drawn four times under a scissor, and the F row is a slab of four bands with black LED windows set in it.
  • The light-grid. A held pad sends its light along its own row and column of the pad grid.
  • The horizon. A chrome line under the voiceprint, with the bars reflected below it at low alpha.

How it reads

Speaks and sounds. Gold against chrome. The voice side is warm, the carrier side is cool, and the hue is never reused for anything else.

Voiceprint. Sixteen columns of dots, 125 Hz at the left, white held peaks, a reflection. The design has horizontal bars: this one turns them upright.

The other modes. TALK: the LPC curve as a line of lit dots along the same wall, gold, chrome when frozen. TUNE: the ladder as rows of dim dots, the pitch a gold block of four, the target rung chrome.

The panel is a true 80 by 24 text screen: 800 / 10 and 480 / 20 land exactly, so every glyph sits on a cell, with yellow for what speaks and cyan for what sounds on a blue field.

What makes it this skin

  • A real grid. The Data face tiles the panel exactly. The columns are 24, 32 and 24 cells, an F key is 10 cells.
  • Titles set into the border. Reverse plates break a double line drawn with rules, because the faces have no box glyphs. A box is 16 words.
  • The prompt line. REC> MIC TO SLOT 1 with a block cursor, the one thing on the screen that blinks.
  • The chord in double size. The Display face set in blocks of two by two cells.

How it reads

Speaks and sounds. A yellow box against a cyan box, drawn with the same double line, and the same two inks all the way down into the meters.

Voiceprint. The design's sixteen horizontal bars, as half-cell rows snapped to 2 dB columns, with a light shade out to the held peak.

The other modes. TALK: the LPC curve as one block a column on the same cell grid. TUNE: the ladder as rows of note names, with > and < closing on the target and a block on the pitch.

Black on white paper, where whatever speaks is printed in solid ink and whatever sounds through a halftone screen, so the idea survives with no colour at all.

What makes it this skin

  • The printed voiceprint. A strip chart: frequency across, time running up, each new row screened to a tone as it prints, with the live bands standing under it as the print head.
  • Meters as scroll bars. IN is the SPEAKS window's own scroll bar with CLIP as its top cap, and OUT is a horizontal bar filled with the checker.
  • Title bars tell the side. SPEAKS is solid, SOUNDS and PADS hatched, the voiceprint checkered, because it is both.
  • One spot colour. Red, for recording, CLIP and a refusal. Nothing else on the screen has a hue.

How it reads

Speaks and sounds. Solid against screened, and filled against outlined. It reads in pure black and white, which is why this skin needs no second ink.

Voiceprint. About 7 seconds of speech as a printed sonagram, so the voice can be read as history rather than as a level. This is the biggest departure from the design's bars.

The other modes. TALK: the strip keeps printing and the LPC curve sits over the head row as a 1 px line. TUNE: the strip prints a pitch chart, one black dot a row on ruled staff lines.

The word counts cover the whole frame, begin to end, without the kit's system strip, which shows for about 3 seconds at a time. Lines of code are estimates. The other figures come from the frames shown above. The 100, 4,800 and 1,350 limits are the ones o3-303's skin plan sets until the unit measures them; 2,048 is the kit's list.

Shipping them together

The seam is six functions: header, F row, panel, chip, pads drawing and voiceprint, chosen by a switch on a skin byte. No heap, no RTTI, and a screen is written once against the seam, so later screens do not multiply by three. The kit's own overlays, HELP and the strip, follow the tokens with nothing added.

The cost that stays is checking: three digests for each of the ten screens, and each skin on the unit once. Switching skins re-uploads that skin's bitmaps to RAM_G.

Notes

  • How the pictures were made. A small model of ui::Renderer and ui::plot::Canvas drew them, using the kit's real glyph bitmaps and contrast pairs, and counted the words and the line load from those same calls. The figures are modelled: neither the simulator nor the unit has run these frames.
  • The faces hold U+0020 to U+007E only. There are no box-drawing or block glyphs, so double lines are rules and blocks are rects. The arrow in the current mock and in the design's capture chip is not in the faces either: the Renderer would draw a question mark. All three skins use >, and the design text needs the same change.
  • Below y 452. The kit's strip covers the bottom 28 px for about 3 seconds, so nothing that matters is drawn there. COLUMN 80's bottom bar holds only hints, which the strip simply replaces.
  • F6. It reads VOICING, as in the mock. Section 4 of the design says CHORD. No skin depends on which is right.
  • The SAMPLE overview. Each skin draws it larger than the design's 384 x 48 bitmap, so each renders one selected slot at its own size. That figure is in each skin's RAM_G line.
  • At phone width the screens are scaled, so rules soften and patterns can shimmer. Judge the patterns at 100%.
